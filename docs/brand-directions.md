# Brand directions

Three different ways Tandoor House could look and feel. Each is scored against the brief; the strongest becomes the design system.

## A. Clay and Ember

**Idea:** the restaurant is its oven. Everything is warm clay, live coals and the arched mouth of the tandoor.

- **Mood:** warm, confident, a little smoky. A busy kitchen seen from a quiet table.
- **Palette, light:** limestone paper `#F7F1E8`, raw clay `#EADFD0`, soot ink `#22180F`, terracotta `#A8461F` (accent), turmeric `#C98A14`, curry leaf green `#3F6B34`.
- **Palette, dark:** banked-coal charcoal `#16110D`, kiln wall `#211A14`, ash cream `#F2E8DA`, ember `#E8784A` (accent, lifted for contrast, not inverted), saffron `#E2B04A`, curry leaf `#86B06F`.
- **Type:** Rozha One for display (a high-contrast serif drawn by an Indian type foundry, with the weight and swing of hand-painted shop signs) and Hanken Grotesk for interface text (open, legible at small sizes, tabular figures for prices).
- **Photos:** close, warm, low-angle. Hands, flame, steam and charred edges. Photos sit inside arched frames on paper, never edge-to-edge on white.
- **Signature element:** the **tandoor arch**: dish photos, the hero and the reservation card are framed in the round-topped arch of the oven mouth, and a hand-drawn ember line underlines active states. With it, a custom line-drawn icon set for the 14 allergens, so the legally required information looks designed, not bolted on.

## B. Bombay Ledger

**Idea:** the old Irani café counter: a cashier's ledger, rubber stamps, bentwood chairs and chequered cloth.

- **Mood:** nostalgic, witty, busy, a city café at 4 pm.
- **Palette, light:** ledger cream `#F4EEDC`, ink black `#1B1A17`, stamp red `#B3261E`, ruled-line teal `#2E6B6B`. **Dark:** ink `#151412`, cream `#EFE6CF`, stamp red lifted `#E0574B`, teal `#6FB3B0`.
- **Type:** a condensed slab for display, a typewriter-flavoured mono for prices, a humanist sans for UI.
- **Photos:** flash-lit, candid, documentary. Black-and-white archive shots next to colour food.
- **Signature element:** rubber-stamp marks and ruled ledger lines: prices sit on ruled lines, status changes are stamped.

## C. Spice Route

**Idea:** the journey of spice: botanical drawings of whole spices and the colours they leave on the cook's hands.

- **Mood:** sensory, botanical, calm luxury.
- **Palette, light:** muslin `#F8F4EC`, cardamom `#5D7A4E`, saffron `#D8962B`, Kashmiri chilli `#9E2B25`, peppercorn `#2B2622`. **Dark:** peppercorn `#141210`, muslin `#EEE6D8`, saffron `#E6AE52`, cardamom `#9DBA8A`.
- **Type:** a soft modern serif for display, a geometric sans for UI.
- **Photos:** overhead, styled, natural light, whole spices scattered around plates.
- **Signature element:** an engraved botanical illustration for each spice that shows up as a category marker and on dish pages.

## Scores

Each criterion out of 5.

| Criterion | A. Clay and Ember | B. Bombay Ledger | C. Spice Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Originality | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| Appetite appeal | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| Legibility | 5 | 4 | 4 |
| Fit for a premium UK restaurant | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| Ease of building well | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| **Total** | **23** | **17** | **17** |

## Choice: A, Clay and Ember

- **It belongs to this restaurant only.** The name is the oven; the arch frame and ember line come straight from it, so the identity cannot be swapped onto another restaurant.
- **Appetite.** Terracotta, turmeric and charcoal are colours of cooked food. Fire and char photograph well in both themes, and the dark theme comes naturally ("banked coals"), so it is designed, not inverted.
- **Premium without fuss.** A big, warm serif and a lot of paper space read as a serious restaurant.
- **It fits the signature motion.** Plates set down, tickets lift, place cards are laid. The arch makes the dish-card-to-dish-page transition easy to follow.
- **Why not B:** the Irani café look is now closely tied to well-known London groups; the brief's own reference study names one. It would be read as borrowed.
- **Why not C:** a botanical illustration for every spice needs an illustrator; done cheaply it looks like clip art. Overhead styled photos also clash with the documentary photo plan.

What A takes from the others: the stamp idea from B returns only as motion (a finished ticket step is stamped), and the curry-leaf green from C marks vegetarian and vegan dishes.
